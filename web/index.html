A calm second pair of eyes for any dashcam

Co-pilot watches the road and quietly hints the driver before things get dangerous: a car cutting in, the car ahead braking hard, a person stepping onto the road, a crash ahead. It is an assistant, not an alarm: most of the time it says nothing.

What it told the driver

    Green — worth watching: a car drifting towards your lane, too close to the car ahead, a driver who already made a risky move.
    Yellow — possible risk: cut-in, hard braking ahead, someone stepping onto the road, a car crossing your path.
    Red — danger: collision about a second away, a crash ahead, a wrong-way car.

    People on the pavement, parked cars and traffic you are not driving into are ignored on purpose. Hints are rationed so the driver does not learn to tune them out.

    How it works

    One camera, no calibration, about ten analyses a second. Only the perception models are learned; every decision after them is geometry we can explain and test.

    1. 01

      See

      YOLO11m, fine-tuned by us on 70 000 BDD100K dashcam frames (40% at night), finds cars, trucks, buses, bikes and people; a ByteTrack-style tracker links them.

    2. 02

      Place

      A lane network (UFLDv2) finds our lane; the horizon and hood are calibrated per camera from the traffic itself; a zebra detector marks crosswalks; optical flow of the far background gives our own turning.

    3. 03

      Predict

      For every road user: time to collision from box growth, lateral speed in lanes per second, and where it will be when we reach it. A constant bearing means a collision course.

    4. 04

      Decide

      Explicit rules turn that into hints: cut-in, crossing, hard braking ahead, pedestrian on our path, crash in view, wrong-way car. Cars that keep making risky moves are remembered and flagged as aggressive drivers. Parked cars and people on the pavement are filtered out.

    5. 05

      Tell

      An advisor picks the colour, the side and one short phrase, with a budget per level: red speaks at once, yellow at most every few seconds, green mostly just glows.

    Results, measured honestly

    Crash clips: the held-out test half of DADA-2000 (never used for tuning); a crash counts as hinted if a yellow or red hint started between 3 s before the labelled start of the danger and the impact. Normal driving: BDD100K and long highway drives — every yellow or red situation there is unnecessary for the driver. "Spoken" is what the driver actually hears after the advisor's budget.

    Known failure cases

    • Loss of control without braking (a car spinning) is not detected as such — only when it ends up in our path.
    • Heavily distorted fisheye cameras: lateral speed is less accurate, so cut-ins are hinted later.
    • Motorbikes weaving between lanes in dense Asian traffic: boxes are small and jumpy; many are hinted only at close range.
    • Without a speed signal from the car, "we are standing still" is inferred from the image; with CAN speed it is exact.
    • Night without street lights: the detector sees headlights rather than cars beyond ~30 m.

    What we measured it on

    • DADA-2000: 1 714 real crash clips from dashcams (China), each labelled with the start of the danger and the crash frame; split into tune / test halves.
    • BDD100K: 150 videos of normal driving (New York, San Francisco; day, night, rain) — every yellow or red hint there counts as false.
    • BDD100K images: 70 000 labelled frames to fine-tune the detector, 10 000 held out for the AP numbers.

    Engineering

    Built to run on the device in the car, not in a cloud.

    Where inference runs

    On the device: the browser (WebGPU / WASM), a laptop (ONNX Runtime with CUDA, DirectML or CPU), or an automotive SoC that imports ONNX. No video leaves the car, no server cost.

    Speed

    Measured on an Intel i5-10400F + RTX 4070 Ti: detector, lanes, crosswalk and logic together ≈ – per analysis at 10 analyses/s. The browser uses a lighter detector so phones keep up.

    Cost per car

    Software only: runs on the head unit or a $40–60 dashcam-class SoC. For a phone app the marginal cost is zero. Detail and payback in the deck.

    Night and rain

    The detector was fine-tuned with 40% night frames; numbers by time of day and weather are in the results above.

    Local roads

    Damas, Nexia, Cobalt, Labo and city buses are ordinary car / bus / truck classes for the detector. Lanes and horizon calibrate per camera, so any mounting works.

    Privacy

    Frames are processed and dropped on the device; nothing is stored or uploaded. Event clips are saved only in the desktop app, locally, for the owner.
